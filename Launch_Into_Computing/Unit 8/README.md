# Unit 8: Principles of Cyber Security

## 📚 Overview

This unit focuses on the principles of cybersecurity and the protection of information systems against threats, vulnerabilities, and unauthorised access.

The work in this unit examines a cybersecurity incident involving the compromise of an employee's credentials through a targeted phishing campaign. The assessment considers how an attacker could use valid credentials to gain unauthorised access to cloud-based business applications and sensitive organisational information.

The assessment also demonstrates the importance of a layered cybersecurity approach, recognising that encryption alone may not prevent an attacker who has successfully authenticated using compromised credentials.

---

## 🔐 Cybersecurity Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan

### Scenario

The assessment examines an enterprise cybersecurity incident in which an attacker obtains valid employee credentials through a targeted phishing campaign.

The compromised credentials are subsequently used to gain unauthorised access to cloud-based business applications.

### Identified Threats and Vulnerabilities

The assessment identifies the following key areas of risk:

- **Compromised credentials** resulting from a targeted phishing attack.
- **Absence of mandatory Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**.
- **Excessive user privileges** and inadequate access management.
- **Insufficient security monitoring and incident detection**.
- Potential unauthorised access to sensitive business and customer information.

---

## 🛡️ Mitigation Strategies

The assessment proposes several security controls to reduce the identified risks.

### Multi-Factor Authentication

Mandatory MFA is recommended for:

- Email
- Cloud applications
- Remote access
- Privileged accounts

MFA provides an additional authentication layer so that a compromised password alone is insufficient to complete authentication.

### Role-Based Access Control and Least Privilege

The assessment recommends implementing role-based access control and the principle of least privilege.

Employees should receive only the permissions required for their responsibilities. Privileged accounts should also be separated from standard user accounts and reviewed regularly.

### Security Monitoring and SIEM

Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) should be implemented to monitor:

- Authentication events
- Administrative activities
- Suspicious access patterns
- Unusual login locations
- Abnormal data access

Effective monitoring can help identify compromised accounts and enable them to be disabled quickly.

### Security Awareness and Phishing Training

Regular employee security-awareness training and simulated phishing exercises are recommended.

The assessment recognises that technical controls should be supported by employee awareness because phishing attacks frequently rely on social engineering.

### Encryption

Encryption remains an important layer of data protection.

Sensitive information should be encrypted to reduce the potential impact of intercepted or unauthorised access to protected data.

However, the assessment highlights that encryption alone cannot prevent an attacker who has successfully authenticated with valid credentials from viewing information that the compromised account is authorised to access.

---

## ⚖️ Compliance, Ethical and Legal Considerations

The assessment considers three important areas:

### Compliance

The organisation should align its information-security controls with **ISO/IEC 27001**.

Where personal data is processed, appropriate technical and organisational measures should be applied to protect that information.

### Ethical Considerations

The organisation has a responsibility to protect employee and customer information while ensuring that security monitoring remains proportionate and transparent.

Employees should be informed about relevant monitoring practices and requirements.

### Legal Considerations

Failure to implement appropriate security measures may expose an organisation to:

- Regulatory penalties
- Contractual consequences
- Legal liability following a data breach

## 🎯 Key Learning Outcomes

This assessment demonstrates the importance of:

- Understanding phishing and credential-based attacks.
- Identifying cybersecurity vulnerabilities.
- Applying layered security controls.
- Implementing Multi-Factor Authentication.
- Applying the principle of least privilege.
- Using security monitoring and SIEM.
- Combining technical controls with security awareness.
- Understanding the role and limitations of encryption.
- Considering compliance, ethical, and legal responsibilities.
